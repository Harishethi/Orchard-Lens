Counting is a tracking problem, not a detection problem.

A camera moving along a row sees the same apple in dozens of frames. Add up every frame's detections and the total balloons. Give each fruit an ID and count it once as it crosses a line, and the number becomes honest.

Why per-frame sums fail

A fruit stays in view for several seconds. Every sample sees it again, so the total grows with how long you film, not with how much fruit there is.

How tracking fixes it

Trackers such as ByteTrack or DeepSORT match boxes between frames using position and motion. Each fruit keeps one ID, and the ID is counted once.

What still goes wrong

Turn off the memory option above. When a leaf hides a fruit at the line, a basic tracker loses its ID and the fruit is never counted. Better trackers keep a lost ID alive for a few frames.

How to tell whether it works.

A good-looking demo proves little. Judge a system on held-out orchards, with the metric that matches the job.

Precision

Of the boxes drawn, what share are real fruit? Low precision means leaves and glints are being counted.

Recall

Of the real fruit, what share was found? This is the number that drives under-counting.

F1 and mAP

F1 balances precision and recall at one threshold. mAP summarises accuracy across all thresholds and box overlaps.

Counting error

Mean absolute error and R-squared against hand counts per tree. This is the check that matters for counting.

Yield error

Percent difference between the forecast and the weighed harvest. The only metric the grower finally cares about.

Published detectors on controlled datasets often report F1 scores in the high 0.8s to low 0.9s. Expect lower in real orchards with heavy leaf cover, so always test on your own rows.

Datasets to start from

MinneApple

Orchard images of apple trees with boxes, segmentation masks and counts. Built for detection, segmentation and counting research.

Laboro Tomato

Tomato images annotated by ripeness stage, which makes it a direct fit for combined detection and maturity work.

Fruits-360

Large collection of single fruit and vegetable images on plain backgrounds. Useful for classification, less so for field detection.

Your own rows

Nothing beats frames from your variety, your trellis and your light. A few hundred well-chosen frames usually matter more than a bigger public set.

What still goes wrong, and what is next.

Fruit detection works well in controlled conditions and gets harder outdoors. These are the problems researchers and growers keep running into.

Hard problems today

  • Occlusion. Leaves, branches and other fruit hide much of the crop. Some fruit is invisible from every side.
  • Lighting. Shadow, glare and dusk change colour, which hurts both detection and ripeness.
  • Small, clustered fruit. Grapes and berries overlap so heavily that boxes stop working.
  • Domain shift. A model trained on one variety, orchard or season often drops in accuracy on another.
  • Labelling cost. Boxing hundreds of fruit per image by hand is slow, and it is where most project time goes.

Where the field is heading

  • Edge inference. Running the model on the tractor, so counts are ready before the row ends.
  • Depth and 3D. RGB-D and multi-view reconstruction to measure size and estimate hidden fruit.
  • Multispectral sensing. Near-infrared and hyperspectral cameras that read sugar and firmness, not just colour.
  • Foundation models. Promptable segmentation and vision-language models that cut labelling effort.
  • Robotic harvest. The same detections guiding a picker arm to ripe fruit only.

Questions people ask first.

Do I need special cameras?

No. A phone or action camera is enough to start. Specialised hardware such as depth or near-infrared cameras becomes worthwhile when you need fruit size or internal quality, not only counts.

How many labelled images do I need?

For one variety in one orchard, a few hundred varied images with a pretrained model is a realistic start. More varieties, seasons and lighting conditions need more, and the improvement usually comes from variety, not volume.

Why does the camera count fewer fruit than the grower?

Some fruit is hidden behind leaves or on the far side of the tree. That is why yield estimates use a correction factor measured against hand counts on a small sample of trees.

Can it tell the variety or detect disease?

The same pipeline can be trained for it, with different labels. This site focuses on detection, counting, maturity and yield.

Are the demos on this page real AI?

They are simulations built to teach how these systems behave. The code samples in the pipeline section use real libraries and can be run on your own footage.
